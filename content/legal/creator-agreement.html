1. What this agreement covers

This agreement applies when you apply to become a Lumina creator and when you submit a film, short, documentary, pilot, series, episode or trailer (“your work”), together with its artwork, subtitles and supporting documents. It supplements the Terms of Service. If the two conflict on a matter about submitted work, this agreement applies.

2. Creator applications

To submit work you first apply with your legal name, a contact email and a short biography, and optionally your company, website, portfolio and country. A member of staff reviews each application and may approve it, decline it or ask for more information. We may decline an application at our discretion, for example when we cannot verify who you are or what you have made before.

3. Your promises about rights

Before you submit, you complete a rights attestation and confirm it. By doing so you promise that, at the time of submission and for as long as your work is available on Lumina:

  1. you are the copyright owner of the work, or hold an exclusive or non-exclusive licence that allows you to grant the rights in this agreement — and you have told us which;
  2. you have cleared every element of the work for streaming in the territories you list: music (composition and recording), archive or stock footage, artwork, fonts, trademarks, and the performances, names and likenesses of everyone who appears in it;
  3. you have disclosed every restriction that applies (for example festival embargoes, territorial limits or end dates);
  4. the work does not infringe anyone’s rights, is not defamatory, and does not break any law in the listed territories;
  5. the information you give us — including titles, credits, synopses, runtimes, languages and release year — is accurate; and
  6. you can provide supporting documents (contracts, licences, releases, cue sheets) if we ask for them.

Your attestation is recorded with the time and the IP address it was made from. If any of these promises stops being true, tell us at [Creator support email] straight away so we can unpublish the work.

4. The licence you grant

When your work is approved and published, you grant [Operator legal name] a non-exclusive, royalty-free licence, for the territories you listed in your attestation (or worldwide if you chose “WW”), to:

  • host, store, transcode and stream the work to Lumina members, in the resolutions, formats and audio and subtitle languages we create from your files;
  • display the title, artwork, synopsis, credits, trailer and short excerpts to describe and promote the work within Lumina; and
  • make copies only as needed to do the above (for example backups and the renditions of an adaptive stream).

The licence does not allow us to sell your work, license it to anyone else, or use it outside Lumina. You keep all ownership of your work and may license it to anyone else at the same time. [Confirm whether the licence should cover any promotion outside the service.]

5. Human review — nothing is published automatically

  • Every submission is reviewed by a member of staff. Status changes are shown in your creator dashboard: submitted, under review, information required, approved, rejected and published.
  • Staff may ask you for more information or documents. You can respond from the submission page; the submission then returns to review.
  • Approval means your work may be prepared for streaming. Staff then create a draft title, complete its artwork, licence and attribution information, and publish it. A submission is never published automatically, and approval does not guarantee publication or a publication date.
  • We may decline any submission, including for technical quality, content standards, unclear rights or fit with the catalog. We will tell you the reason where we can.
  • Messages from staff marked as internal notes are not shown to you; every message addressed to you appears on your submission page and in your notifications.

6. Files and their handling

  • Upload video files for features, episodes and trailers, images for posters and backdrops, subtitles and supporting documents. Size limits are shown in the uploader [currently up to 50 GiB per video file, 15 MB per image, 5 MB per subtitle file and 25 MB per document].
  • Files are stored privately, outside the public website. They are checked by their content, measured (for example resolution, codecs and duration) and may be scanned for malware. Staff open them only through short-lived signed links.
  • An upload that is not completed within 72 hours of its last progress expires and is deleted.
  • To publish, we may convert your video into an adaptive stream (several resolutions). We will not claim a quality, such as 4K, that the delivered renditions do not actually have.

7. Removal

  • You can delete a submission or its files yourself while it is a draft or while we are waiting for information from you.
  • After publication you can ask us to remove your work at any time by writing to [Creator support email]. We will unpublish it within [Removal period]. Lumina does not offer downloads for offline viewing.
  • We may unpublish your work at any time, for example after a valid copyright notice (see the Copyright & Takedown policy), if a rights problem is discovered, or if we stop offering the service.
  • The licence in section 4 ends when your work is unpublished, except for copies kept in backups for up to [Backup retention period] and records we must keep by law.

8. Payment

There are no payment terms at present: Lumina does not pay for submitted work and does not charge creators. If revenue sharing or other payment is offered in future, it will be described in a separate written agreement that you can choose to accept. Nothing in this agreement entitles either party to payment.
