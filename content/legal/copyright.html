1. Our approach

Lumina streams only titles it is entitled to stream. Every title in the catalog carries licence and attribution information on its page: openly licensed works (for example Creative Commons films), works produced for Lumina, and works submitted by creators who have confirmed in writing that they own or control all the rights needed.

1.1 No unlicensed content

Lumina does not link to, embed or proxy unlicensed copies of films or series from other websites, and does not accept submissions that contain material the creator is not entitled to license — including music, footage, artwork, fonts or performances. Creator submissions are reviewed by a person before anything is published, and nothing is published automatically.

Member content — reviews, replies, collection names and notes, and watch-party chat — must also respect other people’s rights. Quoting a short passage of dialogue in a review is usually fine; copying a full screenplay, review or article written by someone else is not.

2. How to report infringement

If you believe material on Lumina infringes a copyright you own or are authorised to act for, send a written notice to our designated agent at [Copyright contact email] or [Postal address]. Signed-in members can also use the “Report” option on a review, reply or shared collection and choose “Copyright”, but a formal notice should still be sent to the agent. Your notice should include:

  1. your name, postal address, telephone number and email address;
  2. identification of the copyrighted work you believe has been infringed (or a representative list if there are several);
  3. identification of the material on Lumina you want removed, with enough detail to find it — ideally the link to the title page, review or collection;
  4. a statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or the law;
  5. a statement that the information in the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury [adapt to [Jurisdiction]], that you are the owner or authorised to act on the owner’s behalf;
  6. your physical or electronic signature.

Notices that are incomplete may take longer to process. Sending a notice you know to be false may make you liable for damages.

3. What happens next

  • We acknowledge complete notices within [Acknowledgement period] and review them promptly.
  • If the notice is valid, we remove or disable access to the material. For a title, that means unpublishing it from the catalog; for a review, reply or collection, hiding or removing it.
  • We tell the member or creator who provided the material that it was removed, give them a copy of the notice (with your contact details removed where the law allows) and explain how to send a counter-notice.
  • Every action is recorded in our internal audit log.

4. Counter-notices

If material you provided was removed and you believe that was a mistake or that you are authorised to use it, you can send a counter-notice to the designated agent. It should include:

  1. your name, address, telephone number and email address;
  2. identification of the material that was removed and where it appeared before removal;
  3. a statement, under penalty of perjury [adapt to [Jurisdiction]], that you have a good-faith belief that the material was removed as a result of a mistake or misidentification;
  4. a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of [Court or jurisdiction for counter-notices] and will accept service of process from the person who sent the original notice;
  5. your physical or electronic signature.

We forward valid counter-notices to the person who sent the original notice. Unless they tell us within [Counter-notice waiting period] that they have started legal proceedings, we may restore the material.

5. Repeat infringers

We terminate, in appropriate circumstances, the accounts of members and creators who repeatedly infringe copyright. As a guide, an account that is the subject of [Number] valid notices within [Period] is suspended and reviewed for termination, and a creator whose submission is found to contain infringing material may lose the ability to submit further work. A suspension signs the account out everywhere.

6. Creators

Creators confirm, before submitting, that they own or have cleared every right in their work. See the Creator Submission Agreement. If a published title is the subject of a valid notice, we unpublish it while the matter is resolved and contact the creator through their submission.

7. Other intellectual property concerns

For trademark or other rights concerns, and for questions about the licence shown on a title page, write to [Legal contact email]. For problems with how a title plays, use the quality report option in the player instead.
